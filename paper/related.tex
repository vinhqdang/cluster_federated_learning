\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
A clustered federated learning method has to settle three questions: which signal the clients provide, how the server turns these signals into clusters and decides how many there are, and what it guarantees or discloses about the result. We follow the taxonomy of the survey by~\citet{benali2026survey}, which separates server-side, client-side and metadata-based methods by the first question, and we organise this section around the three questions. DisCo-CFL is core CFL: it clusters clients to train several models under statistical heterogeneity, as opposed to the operational uses of clustering that the survey also covers.

\paragraph{The signal.} The cheapest source of a signal is what FedAvg already produces. CFL~\citep{sattler2021cfl} bipartitions a cluster by the cosine similarity of the clients' updates once its joint objective is near a stationary point, FL+HC~\citep{briggs2020flhc} clusters the updates hierarchically after a warm-up, and trimmed $k$-means and FeSEM do the same with locally trained weights~\citep{ghosh2019robust,long2023fesem}. Later server-side methods make the comparison cheaper with truncated SVD of the updates~\citep{duan2021flexcfl,yan2023icfl}, greedy merging~\citep{mehta2023flacc}, pairwise penalties~\citep{yu2024fpfc} or gradient trajectories~\citep{liu2024cflgt}. StoCFL~\citep{zeng2025stocfl} compares gradients at a frozen model, as we do, but gradients of the whole model. Client-side methods avoid comparing updates: the server broadcasts $K$ models and each client picks the one with the lowest loss~\citep{mansour2020three,ghosh2020ifca}, or a soft mixture of them~\citep{li2021flsc,ruan2022fedsoft,marfoq2021fedem,guo2024fedrc,guo2025hcfl}. Variants handle drift over time~\citep{jothimurugesan2023feddrift}, add cluster-specific components to a global model~\citep{ma2023fedcam} or adapt local training to imbalanced data~\citep{gong2024autocfl}. The two families share a weakness: their signal entangles what makes clients different, namely the concept, the label proportions and the amount of data. A whole-model update changes with all three and with the round, and a loss favours the model that was trained on a similar label mix. Clients with the same concept but different label proportions are then separated, a behaviour reported for CFL methods~\citep{guo2024fedrc,benali2026survey}. Client-side methods in addition need $K$ in advance, download $K$ models per round and depend on the initialisation.

\paragraph{Stable statistics and class-wise comparison.} Metadata-based methods share instead one statistic that does not move with training: local $k$-means centroids~\citep{dennis2021kfed}, autoencoder embeddings~\citep{jamalirad2022flt}, principal subspaces of the raw data~\citep{vahidian2023pacfl}, predictions on a public set~\citep{morafah2023flis}, label-weighted outputs~\citep{zhang2024mdicfl}, noisy label distributions~\citep{luo2024ppcfl} or other summaries~\citep{ren2025tdcfl,cheng2025snapcfl}. Such statistics are stable and cheap to cluster, but they cost something. A statistic of the inputs alone cannot see a shift in how labels relate to inputs: the subspaces used by PACFL do not change when two labels are swapped, which we observe in our experiments. Statistics that expose data are rated a moderate to high privacy risk by the survey~\citep[Table~4]{benali2026survey}, and only pp-CFL adds a formal privacy mechanism, which protects label distributions rather than the concept. Two recent methods compare clients class by class, which is closest to our idea. FedDAG~\citep{pramanik2026feddag} combines gradient similarity with principal angles between class-wise data subspaces and weights the classes by their frequency, and FedCCFA~\citep{chen2024fedccfa} clusters class-specific classifier vectors under concept drift. DisCo-CFL agrees that concepts should be compared per class, but it compares another object: the expected class-conditional gradient at a frozen model, which depends on $\cP(X\mid Y)$ alone (Proposition~\ref{prop:inv}) and therefore ignores label and quantity skew by construction.

\paragraph{The rule and the number of clusters.} All the methods above fix the number of clusters through a hyperparameter, either $K$ itself or a threshold, an iterative rule or a density parameter such as that of DBSCAN, and the survey names a stable automatic choice of $K$ as an open problem~\citep{benali2026survey}. The difficulty lies less in the clustering algorithm than in the scale of the similarity. When that scale depends on the sample size, the progress of training and the noise, a threshold has no fixed meaning, and the same threshold over-splits clients with little data. The same dependence explains why quantity skew is rarely handled; CORNFLQS~\citep{benali2025cornflqs}, which alternates two clustering signals, is an exception. We take the remedy from measurement theory. Spearman's correction for attenuation~\citep{spearman1904,spearman1910,brown1910}, standard in psychometrics and meta-analysis~\citep{muchinsky1996attenuation}, divides an observed correlation by the reliabilities of the two measurements, so that the corrected value estimates the correlation of the underlying quantities. To our knowledge it has not been used to calibrate similarities between clients. Applied to class-conditional gradients, it gives a statistic that equals one for clients that share a concept whatever the sample size or the noise, so a single threshold has a fixed meaning. Gradient similarity has been used before to group tasks in multi-task learning~\citep{fifty2021tag,yu2020pcgrad}, and the gradient signal-to-noise ratio has been linked to generalisation~\citep{liu2020gsnr}, but neither corrects the similarity for estimation noise.

\paragraph{What else is expected of a grouping decision.} Privacy, fairness and explanations have mostly been studied around the clustering step rather than inside it. Differentially private training clips and perturbs sample or client contributions~\citep{mcmahan2018dpfedavg,abadi2016dpsgd}, secure aggregation hides individual updates~\citep{bonawitz2017secagg} and sketching reduces communication~\citep{rothchild2020fetchsgd}; we apply clipping, sketching and the Gaussian mechanism to the clustering signal itself. Fairness in FL usually means similar accuracy across clients~\citep{li2020qffl,mohri2019afl,li2021ditto}; \citet{mehrabi2021bias} give a broader view of bias. In CFL, unfairness appears earlier, in the grouping: a small group can be absorbed by a larger one, and a small client can be isolated because its statistics are noisy. Explanations that let an affected party understand and contest an automated decision~\citep{wachter2018counterfactual,doshivelez2017interpretable} have, to our knowledge, not been provided for client grouping, and the survey notes that evaluation specific to clustering is rare~\citep[Sec.~4.5.3]{benali2026survey}.

\paragraph{Alternatives to clustering.} Two other families address heterogeneity without clustering. Single-model methods correct client drift~\citep{li2020fedprox,karimireddy2020scaffold}, and personalised FL~\citep{tan2022pflsurvey} adapts one model per client through meta-learning~\citep{fallah2020perfedavg}, regularisation~\citep{tdinh2020pfedme,li2021ditto}, shared representations~\citep{collins2021fedrep} or class prototypes~\citep{tan2022fedproto}. They assume one distribution with local deviations, whereas CFL assumes a few distinct distributions~\citep{benali2026survey}, and we include FedAvg with local fine-tuning as the representative personalisation baseline. Label skew has its own remedies inside a single model, such as logit calibration~\citep{zhang2022fedlc}, in the spirit of label-shift correction~\citep{lipton2018labelshift} and logit adjustment~\citep{menon2021logit}. DisCo-CFL uses the same Bayes argument at inference, within a concept cluster (Proposition~\ref{prop:pc}).

\paragraph{Positioning.} Table~\ref{tab:position} compares representative methods on the properties this paper targets. The entries for other methods summarise what their papers and the survey~\citep[Tables~3 and~5]{benali2026survey} report; ``--'' means that we could not find the property claimed. SA-compat.: compatible with secure aggregation. To our knowledge, DisCo-CFL is the first CFL method that combines (i) a similarity that is invariant to label and quantity skew by construction and calibrated across sample sizes and privacy noise, (ii) an automatic $K$ from a threshold with a fixed meaning, (iii) recovery guarantees independent of group size together with a matching lower bound, machine-checked in Lean~4, and (iv) explanations, certificates and an audit trail of the grouping decision.
