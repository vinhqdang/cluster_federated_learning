\subsection{Realistic benchmarks: natural domain shift and a pretrained network}\label{sec:real}
The experiments above use small CNNs trained from scratch on images with synthetic concept groups. To test the method in a more realistic setting, we repeat the comparison with an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18~\citep{he2016resnet} on two datasets whose clients differ by a \emph{natural} domain shift, and on CIFAR-100.

\paragraph{Setup.} \emph{PACS}~\citep{li2017pacs} has 9{,}991 images of 7 classes in four domains (photo, art painting, cartoon, sketch). \emph{Office-Home}~\citep{venkateswara2017officehome} has 15{,}588 images of 65 classes in four domains (art, clipart, product, real world). Every domain is one concept group, with 10 clients per domain and Dirichlet label skew ($\alpha=0.3$). Clients hold 200 (PACS) or 300 (Office-Home) training images on average; \emph{PACS + QS} adds log-normal quantity skew ($\sigma=1$). \emph{CIFAR-100}~\citep{krizhevsky2009cifar} has 40 clients in four groups, either rotations ($0^\circ$--$270^\circ$) or label swaps (three groups that each swap ten random class pairs, and one group with the original labels). Images are resized to $112\times112$ ($96\times96$ for CIFAR-100). All methods train the same model, the layer-4 block and the classifier of the pretrained ResNet-18 (8.4M parameters). The earlier layers and the batch-normalisation statistics are frozen, so the layer-3 feature maps are computed once. Training uses SGD with learning rate $0.01$, batch size 64, one local epoch and 50 rounds; methods with a warm-up use 10 of them. The learning rate was chosen among $\{0.003,0.01,0.02\}$ on FedAvg, PACS, seed 0 only, before the correction of Section~\ref{sec:setup}, and was not re-selected. DisCo-CFL uses the gradient with respect to the final linear layer, which has a closed form, and all its other hyperparameters are unchanged. IFCA, FeSEM, FL+HC, PACFL and FedDAG-C are given the true number of groups (4); PACFL uses $16\times16$ down-sampled images. Each configuration is run with five seeds; the variants and the ablation with three. For these datasets a conflict means that two clients come from different domains (or rotation or label groups), which is stricter than necessary: two visually similar domains may well be compatible.

\paragraph{Where DisCo-CFL works.} On PACS (Table~\ref{tab:real}) DisCo-CFL recovers the domains in most runs (ARI $0.94\pm0.11$, 1.7\% of the conflicting pairs merged) and matches the Oracle in accuracy (93.3\% against 93.4\%), concept accuracy and worst-group accuracy. The best baselines, PACFL and FL+HC, reach 91.4--91.5\% with ARI at most $0.30$, and FedAvg 90.9\%. With quantity skew (PACS + QS) the picture is the same: 92.6\% against 92.7\% for the Oracle and 90.6\% for the best baseline (PACFL), ARI $0.88$. With label-swap groups on CIFAR-100, DisCo-CFL finds the groups without being told $K$ (ARI $0.96$, 0.7\% conflicting merges) and reaches 57.8\% (Oracle 58.1\%), against 55.8\% for FL+HC, the best baseline, and 53.9\% for FedAvg. The estimated separation is $\hat\Delta=0.21$ on PACS and $0.33$ on CIFAR-100 label swaps (Table~\ref{tab:diag}). These results improve on the original manuscript, where DisCo-CFL only partly separated the PACS domains.

\paragraph{Where it does not.} On Office-Home and on rotated CIFAR-100, DisCo-CFL does not separate the groups: ARI $0.26$ and $0.08$, with 34\% and 60\% of the conflicting pairs merged. The cost in accuracy is small because there is little to gain: the Oracle improves on FedAvg by $1.0$ point on Office-Home and is $1.0$ point \emph{below} FedAvg on rotated CIFAR-100, where the pretrained features make one model sufficient. DisCo-CFL is at the level of FedAvg on Office-Home (69.5\% against 69.4\%) and $1.5$ points below it on rotated CIFAR-100 (51.2\% against 52.7\%), because it trains a few clusters with less data each. No baseline separates these groups either: the best ARI is $0.52$ (FL+HC, given $K$) on Office-Home and $0.47$ (PACFL, given $K$) on rotated CIFAR-100. The diagnostics differ between the two failures. Rotated CIFAR-100 has $\hat\Delta=0.05$, below $1-\tau$: the reference model does not separate rotations of natural images, much as on CIFAR-10. Office-Home has $\hat\Delta=0.13$, just above $1-\tau=0.1$, so the margin $\gamma$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:rec} is about $0.03$ and the finite-sample condition is not met; here Otsu's label-free threshold ($0.94$) recovers more of the structure than the default (ARI $0.70$ against $0.26$, Table~\ref{tab:diag}). Table~\ref{tab:real_variants} reports two remedies (3 seeds). A signature from the gradient of the whole layer-4 block raises ARI to $0.75$ on Office-Home and $0.32$ on rotated CIFAR-100, and a 30-round warm-up to $0.41$ and $0.01$; neither changes accuracy by more than $1.4$ points, consistent with the small gain available.

\paragraph{Ablation on CIFAR-100 label swaps.} Table~\ref{tab:real_ablation} removes one component at a time (3 seeds). Here every main component matters. Without class conditioning, clients are grouped by their label mix: every client is alone ($K=40$), and accuracy falls by 10 points. Without disattenuation, the 100-class signatures are noisy and the federation is split into 16 clusters (ARI $0.26$; accuracy $-7.2$ points), which supports H3 more clearly than the Fashion-MNIST ablation. With the mean instead of the bottleneck, the swaps, which touch 20 of the 100 classes, are averaged away and all groups are merged ($K=1.3$, 96\% conflicting merges; $-3.7$ points), which supports H4. Linkage-ordered merging over-splits ($K=7.7$, ARI $0.68$); clipping and attachment make little difference.

\paragraph{Statistical comparison.} Over the five realistic benchmarks, the hierarchical bootstrap gives DisCo-CFL a positive mean difference in accuracy against every baseline (Table~\ref{tab:hier}, part b), with intervals that exclude zero except against FedAvg ($+1.4$, $[-0.3,+3.1]$). With five scenarios the smallest possible Wilcoxon $p$-value is $0.031$, so these tests have little power, and the per-benchmark results above are the more informative summary: clear gains on PACS and CIFAR-100 label swaps, none on Office-Home, and a small loss on rotated CIFAR-100.

\paragraph{FEMNIST: natural writers without ground-truth groups.} FEMNIST~\citep{caldas2018leaf} has handwritten characters (62 classes) from thousands of writers. We take 100 writers with at least 150 samples and train the small CNN of the earlier experiments from scratch. There is no true number of groups, so IFCA, FeSEM, FL+HC and FedDAG-C are given $K=4$ without justification. DisCo-CFL forms one cluster in four of the five seeds and two in the fifth, and its accuracy equals that of FedAvg (62.8\%; Table~\ref{tab:real_femnist}). Methods that split the writers into a few clusters are 1--2 points more accurate on local test data (IFCA 65.2\%, FeSEM 64.6\%, FedDAG-C and StoCFL-W 64.5\%), but generalise less to other writers (pooled accuracy 50.7--55.3\% against 58.6\% for DisCo-CFL and 58.7\% for FedAvg). With a reference model of 10 rounds, the writers' class-conditional gradients are not separated enough for DisCo-CFL to split them, so on natural writer heterogeneity it reduces to FedAvg. This reverses the behaviour reported in the original manuscript (47 clusters), which was an artefact of the implementation error.
