\subsection{Setup}
\paragraph{Data and heterogeneity.} We build federations from Fashion-MNIST (main), MNIST and CIFAR-10 following the non-IID taxonomy used in the survey~\citep{benali2026survey}. Each client belongs to a latent concept group. \emph{Concept shift on features} is a rotation of the inputs by $0^\circ,90^\circ,180^\circ,270^\circ$. \emph{Concept shift on targets} is a label permutation that swaps two pairs of classes per group. On top of this, every client has \emph{target skew} (Dirichlet label proportions, $\alpha=0.3$, or $\alpha=0.1$ in the label-only scenario) and, where indicated, \emph{quantity skew} (log-normal dataset sizes with $\sigma=1$, clipped to $[30,3200]$, mean 400). The scenarios are:
\emph{Rotation} (4 groups $\times$ 10 clients), \emph{Label swap} (4$\times$10), \emph{Rotation + QS}, \emph{Mixed + QS} (2 rotations $\times$ 2 labelings, so that some groups differ only on 4 classes), \emph{Label skew only} (a single concept, 40 clients; the correct answer is $K=1$), and \emph{Minority groups} (rotation groups with $25/9/4/2$ clients). Each client has a local test set drawn with its own label proportions and transformation. We also evaluate every model on a class-balanced test set of its client's concept (\emph{concept accuracy}), which measures generalisation beyond the local label mix.

\paragraph{Training.} All methods use the same CNN (two convolutional and two fully connected layers, $d=$\,46{,}730 parameters for $28\times28$ inputs, 65{,}962 for CIFAR-10), SGD with learning rate $0.05$, batch size 32, one local epoch per round, full participation and 50 communication rounds. Methods with a warm-up (DisCo-CFL, FL+HC, Oracle) spend 10 of the 50 rounds on FedAvg. \emph{Local} trains each client alone for 50 epochs. Every configuration is run with three seeds, which change the federation, the initialisation and the sketch.

\paragraph{Baselines.} FedAvg~\citep{mcmahan2017fedavg}; Local; IFCA~\citep{ghosh2020ifca} (client-side); CFL/MTCFL~\citep{sattler2021cfl} (server-side recursive cosine bipartitioning, triggered when the norm of the aggregated update falls below $\varepsilon_1=0.9$ times the mean client-update norm; $\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2$ and the cross-similarity check were selected on the same pilot run as DisCo-CFL); FL+HC~\citep{briggs2020flhc} (Ward clustering of local updates after the warm-up); PACFL~\citep{vahidian2023pacfl} (metadata-based, principal angles between client data subspaces, $p=3$). IFCA, FL+HC and PACFL are \emph{given the true number of groups} $K$, which favours them; DisCo-CFL and MTCFL find $K$ on their own. \emph{Oracle} trains on the ground-truth groups and is an upper reference.

\paragraph{DisCo-CFL.} Sketch dimension $p=1024$, at most 200 samples per class for the signature, classes with fewer than 4 samples not reported, $\tau=0.8$, $\lambda=0.5$, $e_{\min}=0.05$, low-evidence threshold $0.3$, $z=2$. These values, and the design choices of bottleneck linkage and evidence-ordered merging, were fixed during pilot runs on seed 0 of the Rotation, Label swap, Mixed + QS and label-only configurations (and seed 1 of Label swap with QS), using clustering quality only. They are then kept identical for all scenarios, seeds and datasets; seeds 1 and 2 and the MNIST/CIFAR-10 results were not used for design. Section~\ref{sec:sens} reports the sensitivity to $\tau$ and $\lambda$.

\paragraph{Metrics.} \emph{Performance:} mean local test accuracy (Acc). \emph{Fairness:} accuracy of the worst 10\% of clients (Acc@10\%), accuracy of the worst concept group (Worst-grp), Jain's index. \emph{Clustering:} ARI with respect to the groups, number of clusters $K$, and the \emph{conflict-merge rate}: the fraction of conflicting client pairs (Definition~\ref{def:concept}) that end up in the same cluster. ARI penalises the legitimate placement of compatible clients (e.g.\ a client of a label-swap group that holds none of the swapped classes), whereas the conflict-merge rate measures exactly the harmful errors.
