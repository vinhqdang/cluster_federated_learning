\subsection{Setup}\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Benchmarks.} We use three families of benchmarks, listed in Table~\ref{tab:scenarios}. Federations follow the non-IID taxonomy of the survey~\citep{benali2026survey}, which lists image classification with rotations and label swaps as the standard CFL benchmark~\citep[Table~6]{benali2026survey}.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{Small CNNs trained from scratch} (Sections~\ref{sec:small} and~\ref{sec:extra}): MNIST~\citep{lecun1998mnist}, Fashion-MNIST~\citep{xiao2017fmnist} (the main benchmark, on which all scenario types and sweeps are run), KMNIST~\citep{clanuwat2018kmnist}, EMNIST~\citep{cohen2017emnist} (balanced split, 47 classes), SVHN~\citep{netzer2011svhn} (photographs of house numbers) and CIFAR-10~\citep{krizhevsky2009cifar} (object images), with synthetic concept groups.
\item \emph{A pretrained ResNet-18}~\citep{he2016resnet} (Sections~\ref{sec:real} and~\ref{sec:extra}) on two datasets whose clients differ by a \emph{natural} domain shift and on two datasets with synthetic groups. \emph{PACS}~\citep{li2017pacs} has 9{,}991 images of 7 classes in four domains (photo, art painting, cartoon, sketch); \emph{Office-Home}~\citep{venkateswara2017officehome} has 15{,}588 images of 65 classes in four domains (art, clipart, product, real world). Every domain is one concept group of 10 clients. \emph{CIFAR-100}~\citep{krizhevsky2009cifar} and \emph{CIFAR-10} have 40 clients in four rotation or label-swap groups (for CIFAR-100, three groups that each swap ten random class pairs and one group with the original labels).
\item \emph{Two benchmarks of other kinds.} \emph{FEMNIST}~\citep{caldas2018leaf} (Section~\ref{sec:real}): handwritten characters (62 classes) of 100 writers with at least 150 samples each, a natural heterogeneity without ground-truth groups, with the small CNN. \emph{AG News}~\citep{zhang2015charcnn} (Section~\ref{sec:extra}): news topic classification (4 classes) with a frozen pretrained sentence encoder~\citep{reimers2019sbert} and a trainable two-layer classifier head; four label-swap groups (the original labels and three different swaps of two classes).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Heterogeneity.} Each client belongs to a latent concept group. \emph{Concept shift on features} is a rotation of the inputs (by $0^\circ,90^\circ,180^\circ,270^\circ$, or by $0,\theta,2\theta,3\theta$ in the strength sweep) or, for PACS and Office-Home, the domain. \emph{Concept shift on targets} is a label permutation that swaps two pairs of classes per group unless stated otherwise. On top of this, every client has \emph{target skew} (Dirichlet label proportions with $\alpha=0.3$; $\alpha=0.1$ in the label-only scenario and $\alpha=0.5$ for CIFAR-10 with the pretrained network and AG News) and, where indicated, \emph{quantity skew} (log-normal dataset sizes with $\sigma=1$, clipped to $[30,3200]$). Clients hold 400 training samples on average with the small CNN, 200 (PACS) or 300 (Office-Home, CIFAR, AG News) otherwise. Each client has a local test set drawn with its own label proportions and transformation. We also evaluate every model on a class-balanced test set of its client's concept (\emph{concept accuracy}), which measures generalisation beyond the local label mix. For the realistic benchmarks a conflict means that two clients come from different domains or groups, which is stricter than necessary: two visually similar domains may well be compatible.

\paragraph{Training.} All methods use the same CNN (two convolutional and two fully connected layers; $d=46{,}730$ parameters for $28\times28$ inputs and $65{,}962$ for $32\times32$ colour images), stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with learning rate $0.05$, batch size 32, one local epoch per round and 50 communication rounds. Methods with a warm-up (DisCo-CFL, FL+HC, FeSEM, Oracle) spend 10 of the 50 rounds on FedAvg. \emph{Local} trains each client alone for 50 epochs. With the pretrained ResNet-18, all methods train the layer-4 block and the classifier (8.4M parameters); the earlier layers and the batch-normalisation statistics are frozen, so the layer-3 feature maps are computed once. Images are resized to $112\times112$ (PACS, Office-Home) or $96\times96$ (CIFAR), and training uses SGD with learning rate $0.01$ and batch size 64; the learning rate was chosen among $\{0.003,0.01,0.02\}$ with FedAvg on PACS, seed 0 only. On AG News the head is trained with learning rate $0.1$ and batch size 64 on standardised embeddings, chosen with FedAvg on a single-concept federation before any other method was run. DisCo-CFL then uses the gradient with respect to the final linear layer, which has a closed form; all its other hyperparameters are the same on every benchmark. All main scenarios are run with five seeds; the sweeps, the ablations, the variants of DisCo-CFL on the realistic benchmarks and the clustering-level studies with three. A seed changes the federation, the initialisation and the sketch. Section~\ref{sec:cost} reports wall-clock time and communication for every method.

\paragraph{Baselines.} We compare with twelve methods that cover all three families of the taxonomy, the three closest class-wise or frozen-model methods, and the main alternatives to clustering:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{Single model / personalisation:} FedAvg~\citep{mcmahan2017fedavg}; Local; FedAvg-FT (FedAvg followed by one epoch of local fine-tuning, a standard personalisation baseline).
\item \emph{Client-side:} IFCA~\citep{ghosh2020ifca}.
\item \emph{Server-side:} CFL/MTCFL~\citep{sattler2021cfl} (recursive cosine bipartitioning, triggered when the norm of the aggregated update falls below $\varepsilon_1=0.9$ times the mean client-update norm); FL+HC~\citep{briggs2020flhc} (Ward clustering of local updates after the warm-up); FeSEM~\citep{long2023fesem} (expectation--maximisation-like assignment to the nearest of $K$ centres in parameter space, with the same warm-up and a $k$-means++ initialisation on the clients' local models).
\item \emph{Metadata-based:} PACFL~\citep{vahidian2023pacfl} (principal angles between client data subspaces, $p=3$).
\item \emph{Closest methods}, reimplemented from the papers with the same model, optimiser, data and number of rounds as all other methods: StoCFL~\citep{zeng2025stocfl} (normalised full-batch gradients at the initial model, greedy merging while the cosine exceeds $\tau_{\rm S}=0.5$, bi-level training with a proximal weight $0.05$; two local epochs per round, one for the cluster model and one for the global model); StoCFL-W, the same with the gradients taken at the 10-round warm-up model that DisCo-CFL uses, so that the two differ only in the signal and the rule; FedCCFA~\citep{chen2024fedccfa} (shared feature extractor, per-class clustering of balanced classifier rows by DBSCAN on the mean absolute difference of distances (MADD) with radius $0.1$ and one sample per core, classifier averaging within the per-class clusters, and prototype alignment; each client predicts with its personal model, i.e.\ the shared extractor and its classifier after the local classifier epoch of the protocol); and FedDAG-C, the clustering stage of FedDAG~\citep{pramanik2026feddag} (gradient similarity after two local epochs fused with frequency-weighted principal angles between class-wise data subspaces, average linkage with the true $K$, then FedAvg within clusters). FedCCFA clusters each class separately; for ARI we use the partition of clients induced by identical cluster memberships over all classes. The deviations from the original papers (a single SGD optimiser for all methods; for FedDAG-C, a fixed fusion weight instead of its adaptive weighting and the true $K$ instead of an index-based choice) are the only ones.
\item \emph{Oracle:} FedAvg inside the ground-truth groups (an upper reference).
\end{itemize}
IFCA, FL+HC, FeSEM, PACFL and FedDAG-C are \emph{given the true number of groups} $K$, which favours them (on FEMNIST, which has no true $K$, they are given $K=4$; PACFL uses $16\times16$ down-sampled images on the realistic benchmarks, and PACFL and FedDAG-C, which need image inputs, are not run on AG News). DisCo-CFL, MTCFL, StoCFL and FedCCFA find $K$ on their own. The StoCFL threshold ($\tau_{\rm S}\in\{0.1,0.3,0.5,0.7,0.8,0.9\}$, at the initial and at the warm-up model) and the FedCCFA radius ($\{0.02,0.05,0.1,0.2,0.4\}$) were selected on the same pilot runs as the threshold of DisCo-CFL; the MTCFL thresholds and the FeSEM initialisation were selected on an earlier set of pilot runs and not re-selected with it, an asymmetry in favour of DisCo-CFL. No StoCFL threshold reaches a mean pilot ARI above $0.1$, so we keep the published default $0.5$; for FedCCFA the published default $0.1$ has the highest mean pilot ARI. \emph{Budgets.} All methods use 50 rounds of one local epoch per client, except StoCFL (two epochs per round) and FedDAG-C (two warm-up epochs); warm-up rounds count towards the 50. Hyperparameters of the baselines were otherwise taken from their papers, without further tuning for any method.

\paragraph{DisCo-CFL.} Sketch dimension $p=1024$, at most 200 samples per class for the signature, classes with fewer than 4 samples not reported, $\tau=0.9$, $\lambda=0.5$, $e_{\min}=0.05$, low-evidence threshold $0.3$, $z=2$, per-sample clipping $C_{\rm clip}=0.5$. These values, and the design choices of bottleneck linkage and evidence-ordered merging, were fixed during pilot runs on seed 0 of the Rotation, Label swap, Mixed + QS and label-only configurations using clustering quality \emph{measured against the ground-truth groups}, which a deployment would not have. They are kept identical for all scenarios, seeds and datasets. No other seed, sweep or dataset was used for design. Per-sample clipping ($C_{\rm clip}$ chosen on seed 0 only, from $\{0.5,1,2\}$ in all scenarios and additionally $\{0.1,0.25\}$ in the label-only scenario) was added after a first sensitivity study showed over-splitting caused by heavy-tailed per-sample gradients. The run without clipping is reported as an ablation. \emph{Threshold.} $\tau$ and the warm-up length were selected on the pilot runs (seed 0 of the four configurations, $\tau\in\{0.70,0.75,0.80,0.85,0.90\}$, $T_0\in\{10,20,30\}$): $\tau=0.9$ with $T_0=10$ gives ARI $0.93$, $1.00$, $1.00$ and $1.00$, whereas $\tau=0.8$ merges groups (ARI $0.60$ on Rotation and $0.64$ on Mixed + QS). This value lies close to an edge: it is the largest value of the sensitivity grid at which the label-only federation is never split, and at $\tau=0.95$ it is split in one of three seeds (Section~\ref{sec:sens}). \emph{Development and evaluation runs.} The pilot runs (seed 0 of Rotation, Label swap, Mixed + QS and label-only, and seed 0 of PACS for the learning rate of the pretrained network) are development runs. All results include them; the hierarchical comparison is repeated on the held-out runs only, which exclude them (Table~\ref{tab:hier_heldout}). On the colour datasets and the realistic benchmarks we also report DisCo-T30, which uses 30 warm-up rounds. Section~\ref{sec:sens} reports the sensitivity to $\tau$ and $\lambda$.

\paragraph{Studies specified in advance.} Six studies were specified before any of their runs: their scenarios, hypotheses and decision criteria were fixed and recorded with a time stamp (a record of our own, not an entry in an external registry). They are the four additional benchmarks of Section~\ref{sec:extra}, the rerun with the shrinkage covered by the theory (Section~\ref{sec:sep}), the label-free warm-up rule (Section~\ref{sec:warmup}), the dependence on the reference model and the concept-drift test (Section~\ref{sec:fat-exp}), and the simulations of the certificate standard errors (Section~\ref{sec:outputs}). The warm-up rule was designed after the failures it addresses had been observed. The hypotheses of each study are stated where it is reported, and we report the outcome of every one, including those that fail; analyses without a prior hypothesis are called exploratory where they appear.

\paragraph{Metrics.} \emph{Performance:} mean local test accuracy (Acc), with the prior correction of Section~\ref{sec:pc} applied identically to every method (for methods that predict with per-client models, such as Local, FedAvg-FT and FedCCFA, the cluster prior equals the client's own prior and the correction has no effect), and concept accuracy. \emph{Fairness:} accuracy of the worst 10\% of clients (Acc@10\%), accuracy of the worst concept group (Worst-grp), Jain's index. \emph{Clustering:} adjusted Rand index (ARI) with respect to the groups, number of clusters $K$, and the \emph{conflict-merge rate}: the fraction of conflicting client pairs (Definition~\ref{def:concept}) that end up in the same cluster. ARI penalises the legitimate placement of compatible clients (e.g.\ a client of a label-swap group that holds none of the swapped classes), whereas the conflict-merge rate measures exactly the harmful errors. \emph{Statistics.} Every number in this paper comes from actual runs of the methods as described here. Runs with different seeds of the same scenario share the scenario, so (scenario, seed) pairs are not independent. We report, per dataset, means with 95\% $t$-intervals over seeds and \emph{failure rates} (fraction of runs with ARI below $0.5$ when there are several groups). For comparisons across scenarios we use (i) a hierarchical bootstrap that resamples scenarios and then seeds within scenarios~\citep{saravanan2020hierarchical} ($10^4$ replicates) for the mean difference between DisCo-CFL and each baseline, and (ii) one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on scenario-level means, one value per scenario~\citep{demsar2006statistical}. The hierarchical comparison is also reported on held-out runs.
