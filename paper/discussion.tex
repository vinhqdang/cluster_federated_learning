\paragraph{What is new, and what it delivers.} Earlier CFL methods cluster whatever the clients send (updates, losses, data statistics) with a similarity whose scale depends on sample size, on training progress and on noise. DisCo-CFL defines the clustering target precisely (class-conditional concept equivalence) and estimates a similarity whose population value at a fixed reference model is known in advance for clients that share a concept, namely $1$. The evidence for the five hypotheses of the introduction is as follows. \emph{H1} holds: under label and quantity skew alone, DisCo-CFL forms one cluster, whereas StoCFL and MTCFL split. \emph{H2} holds where its condition holds with a margin: when the estimated separation clearly exceeds $1-\tau$ (Fashion-MNIST, MNIST, KMNIST, PACS, CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 label swaps with a pretrained network; $\hat\Delta\ge0.2$), DisCo-CFL recovers the groups and matches the oracle; when it is below $1-\tau$ (SVHN, CIFAR-10, the $15^\circ$ rotation) it fails. Office-Home and AG News ($\hat\Delta=0.13$, ARI $0.26$ and $0.79$) show that a separation just above $1-\tau$ is not enough, and EMNIST label swaps (47 classes) that noisy signatures can split a separated group: the finite-sample margin $\gamma=\tau-1+\Delta$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:rec} is then only about $0.03$. \emph{H3} holds in a weaker form than we expected: disattenuation makes the threshold much less sensitive and is essential under DP noise, but at the default threshold on clean data it adds only $0.04$--$0.11$ ARI. \emph{H4} holds: the mean over classes merges label-swap groups that the bottleneck separates. \emph{H5} holds partly: explanations never report a wrong divergent class but miss classes on which concepts are close, and confident certificates are right in 92--100\% of the cases.

\paragraph{Three kinds of concept structure.} Our evidence is of three kinds, which should not be confused. (i) \emph{Recoverable class-conditional concepts}: groups whose class-conditional gradients differ at the reference model by more than $1-\tau$. Theorem~\ref{thm:rec} applies, and the experiments confirm recovery. (ii) \emph{Synthetic ground truth}: rotations and label swaps imposed on public datasets. They let us measure recovery, but they are of type (i) only if the reference model separates them, which fails for SVHN and CIFAR-10 with a 10-round warm-up and for rotated CIFAR-100 with a pretrained network. (iii) \emph{Natural heterogeneity} such as domains (PACS, Office-Home) or writers (FEMNIST). Its concept structure is not unique: two visually similar domains may well be compatible, and handwriting forms a continuum. ARI against domains is then only a proxy, and the meaningful question is whether clustering improves accuracy, which it does on PACS and does not on Office-Home and FEMNIST.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
(i) \emph{Choice of the reference model.} The method is only as good as its reference model. With 10 warm-up rounds it fails completely on SVHN and CIFAR-10 (up to 17 points below the oracle), and 30 rounds repair SVHN label swaps only. A pre-registered label-free rule (DisCo-AS, Section~\ref{sec:datasets}) that waits until the model has learned and the partition is stable repairs SVHN label swaps and does no harm elsewhere, but within a 50-round budget it does not repair SVHN rotations or CIFAR-10.
(ii) \emph{Threshold.} $\tau=0.9$ was chosen on pilot runs scored against the ground truth. A label-free threshold from Otsu's criterion recovers label swaps but merges rotation groups.
(iii) \emph{Privacy.} The DP guarantee covers the signature for same-label record replacement given the reference model. The reference model is trained on the same clients without DP in our experiments, so the end-to-end release is not private; the class support and the per-class counts are revealed, and padding hides them only weakly. Under DP noise, recovery is partial in all our settings (ARI at most $0.76$ with 1{,}500 samples per client at $\varepsilon=10$).
(iv) \emph{Guarantees.} The analysis covers the merging stage under independent sub-Gaussian per-sample signatures, a fixed reference model, separation, coverage and evidence. It does not cover attachment, our $\lambda=0.5$ violates the condition of Corollary~\ref{cor:sc}, and the constants are not optimised. The certificates' standard errors have the right scale, but the minimum over classes is biased downwards, so the nominal coverage is not achieved.
(v) \emph{Coverage and small clients.} Clients that hold none of the discriminative classes are compatible with several groups; with extreme label skew ($\alpha=0.1$) ARI falls to about $0.7$. With small clients (100 samples) ARI also falls to about $0.7$, mainly because the groups become closer at a reference model trained on less data.
(vi) \emph{Scope of the properties.} Fairness here means that the clustering decision does not depend on group size and that worst-group accuracy is high; it does not cover other notions of fairness. Explanations are faithful only for classes with enough evidence. There is no defence against malicious clients: random or sign-flipped signatures are flagged or isolated, but a client that copies another group's signature is merged into that group undetected.
(vii) \emph{One-shot clustering.} Concept drift after clustering would require recomputing signatures, although the newcomer test already detects clients whose concept is unseen.
(viii) \emph{Experimental scale.} The realistic benchmarks have four groups and 40 clients and use one pretrained network.

\paragraph{Verifiable next steps.} Three strategies follow from the results, and each can be checked on held-out data with the diagnostics we report. \emph{Threshold selection}: a label-free threshold from the distribution of pairwise similarities (Otsu's $\hat\tau$) recovers label swaps but merges rotation groups, and its separation index $\eta$ does not separate our successes from our failures ($\eta=0.63$ for the $45^\circ$ rotation with ARI $1.00$, $0.73$ for CIFAR-10 label swaps with ARI $0.28$). A usable rule needs a statistic that is calibrated against the reliability of the signatures, e.g.\ a permutation test of the gap between within- and between-cluster similarities, and must be evaluated on runs not used to design it. \emph{Adaptive warm-up}: the stable warm-up rule DisCo-AS (Section~\ref{sec:datasets}) is a first such strategy, validated on all small-CNN scenarios; it should be tested with larger round budgets and on the realistic benchmarks, where pretrained models learn quickly. \emph{Concept drift}: recomputing the signatures at the same reference model detects every drifted client in our test (Section~\ref{sec:fat-exp}), but the one-step test raises many false alarms because drifted clients remain in their old clusters; an iterative rule that first removes clients whose certificate turned uncertain, and then retests the others against the remaining members, brought false alarms to 1--4\% in an exploratory analysis and should be validated on new data.
