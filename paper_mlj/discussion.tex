\paragraph{What is new, and what it delivers.} Earlier CFL methods cluster whatever the clients send (updates, losses, data statistics) with a similarity whose scale depends on sample size, on training progress and on noise. DisCo-CFL defines the clustering target precisely (class-conditional concept equivalence) and estimates a similarity whose population value at a fixed reference model is known in advance for clients that share a concept, namely $1$. The evidence for the five hypotheses of the introduction is as follows. \emph{H1} holds: under label and quantity skew alone, DisCo-CFL forms one cluster, whereas StoCFL and MTCFL split. \emph{H2} holds where its condition holds: when the estimated separation exceeds $1-\tau$ (Fashion-MNIST, MNIST, PACS, CIFAR-100 label swaps), DisCo-CFL recovers the groups and matches the oracle; when it does not, it fails. \emph{H3} holds in a weaker form than we expected: disattenuation makes the threshold much less sensitive and is essential under DP noise, but at the default threshold on clean data it adds only $0.04$--$0.11$ ARI. \emph{H4} holds: the mean over classes merges label-swap groups that the bottleneck separates. \emph{H5} holds partly: explanations never report a wrong divergent class but miss classes on which concepts are close, and confident certificates are right in 92--100\% of the cases.

\paragraph{Three kinds of concept structure.} Our evidence is of three kinds, which should not be confused. (i) \emph{Recoverable class-conditional concepts}: groups whose class-conditional gradients differ at the reference model by more than $1-\tau$. Theorem~\ref{thm:rec} applies, and the experiments confirm recovery. (ii) \emph{Synthetic ground truth}: rotations and label swaps imposed on public datasets. They let us measure recovery, but they are of type (i) only if the reference model separates them, which fails for SVHN and CIFAR-10 with a 10-round warm-up and for rotated CIFAR-100 with a pretrained network. (iii) \emph{Natural heterogeneity} such as domains (PACS, Office-Home) or writers (FEMNIST). Its concept structure is not unique: two visually similar domains may well be compatible, and handwriting forms a continuum. ARI against domains is then only a proxy, and the meaningful question is whether clustering improves accuracy, which it does on PACS and does not on Office-Home and FEMNIST.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
(i) \emph{Choice of the reference model.} The method is only as good as its reference model. With 10 warm-up rounds it fails completely on SVHN and CIFAR-10 (up to 17 points below the oracle), and 30 rounds repair SVHN label swaps only. We do not have a label-free rule that sets the warm-up length; a loss-plateau rule stops too early because the loss is also flat before learning starts.
(ii) \emph{Threshold.} $\tau=0.9$ was chosen on pilot runs scored against the ground truth, after an earlier value ($0.8$) stopped working when we corrected an implementation error. A label-free threshold from Otsu's criterion recovers label swaps but merges rotation groups.
(iii) \emph{Privacy.} The DP guarantee covers the signature for same-label record replacement given the reference model. The reference model is trained on the same clients without DP in our experiments, so the end-to-end release is not private; the class support and the per-class counts are revealed, and padding hides them only weakly. Under DP noise, recovery is partial unless clients hold well over 1{,}000 samples.
(iv) \emph{Guarantees.} The analysis covers the merging stage under independent sub-Gaussian per-sample signatures, a fixed reference model, separation, coverage and evidence. It does not cover attachment, our $\lambda=0.5$ violates the condition of Corollary~\ref{cor:sc}, and the constants are not optimised. The certificates' standard errors have the right scale, but the minimum over classes is biased downwards, so the nominal coverage is not achieved.
(v) \emph{Coverage.} Clients that hold none of the discriminative classes are compatible with several groups, and with extreme label skew ($\alpha=0.1$) or small clients ARI falls to about $0.7$.
(vi) \emph{Scope of the properties.} Fairness here means that the clustering decision does not depend on group size and that worst-group accuracy is high; it does not cover other notions of fairness. Explanations are faithful only for classes with enough evidence. Robustness to malicious clients is not studied.
(vii) \emph{One-shot clustering.} Concept drift after clustering would require recomputing signatures, although the newcomer test already detects clients whose concept is unseen.
(viii) \emph{Experimental scale.} The realistic benchmarks have four groups and 40 clients and use one pretrained network.

\paragraph{Verifiable next steps.} Three strategies follow from the results, and each can be checked on held-out data with the diagnostics we report. \emph{Threshold selection}: set $\tau$ per run from the bimodality of the pairwise similarities (Otsu's $\hat\tau$ and its separation index $\eta$) and accept the clustering only when $\eta$ indicates two modes; our results show that $\eta$ is high where recovery succeeds and low for the $15^\circ$ rotations and FEMNIST. \emph{Adaptive warm-up}: continue FedAvg until a measure of learning progress, such as the relative decrease of the training loss since the first round, reaches a fixed fraction, and then until the median split-half reliability and $\eta$ stop changing; this must be validated on runs that were not used to design it. \emph{Concept drift}: recompute the signatures at the same reference model at regular intervals and apply the newcomer test to every client; a client whose certificate turns from confident to uncertain or novel is a candidate for reassignment.
