\subsection{Additional benchmarks: CIFAR-10 with a pretrained network, text, KMNIST and EMNIST}\label{sec:extra}
Seven scenarios on four datasets that were not used to develop the method test whether its scope, as stated by the results above, carries over. CIFAR-10 with the pretrained ResNet-18 also tests the explanation of the small-CNN failure on CIFAR-10 (an uninformative reference model). The predictions were: DisCo-CFL recovers the groups (mean ARI $\ge0.8$) on CIFAR-10 label swaps with the pretrained network, with accuracy within one point of the oracle (H-A1), on AG News (H-B1) and in all four KMNIST and EMNIST scenarios (H-C1); no success was predicted for CIFAR-10 rotations, which the pretrained features were expected not to separate, as on CIFAR-100. \paragraph{Outcomes against the predictions.} Of the three hypotheses of success, H-A1 (CIFAR-10 label swaps with the pretrained network) holds, and H-B1 (AG News) and H-C1 (all four KMNIST and EMNIST scenarios) fail; at the level of scenarios, four of the six predicted recoveries occur. The predicted failure on CIFAR-10 rotations occurs. (i) On CIFAR-10 with label swaps and the pretrained network, DisCo-CFL recovers the groups (ARI $0.99$, $\hat\Delta=0.34$) and equals the oracle (88.3\%), against 86.2\% for the best baseline (MTCFL); the best baseline ARI is $0.64$ (FL+HC, given $K$). This is consistent with the diagnosis of the small-CNN failure on CIFAR-10: with an informative reference model the same label-swap groups are recovered. The comparison changes both the architecture and the pretraining, and the image size, so it does not isolate the reference model as the cause. (ii) On CIFAR-10 rotations the pretrained features do not separate the groups ($\hat\Delta=0.02$) and DisCo-CFL fails (ARI $0.07$), as predicted from rotated CIFAR-100; its accuracy is 0.5 points below the best baseline (PACFL, given $K$). (iii) On KMNIST (rotations and label swaps) and EMNIST rotations, DisCo-CFL recovers the groups (ARI $0.95$--$0.98$) and is within $0.3$ points of the oracle. (iv) Two predictions fail. On EMNIST label swaps ARI is $0.56$: the groups are separated ($\hat\Delta=0.32$), but with 47 classes the per-class signatures are noisy, the smallest pooled linkage within a true group is $0.88<\tau$, and DisCo-CFL over-splits (5.6 clusters, 2.2\% of the conflicting pairs merged). Its accuracy (83.7\%) is still the highest among non-oracle methods, 1.1 points below the oracle. On AG News ARI is $0.79$, just below the predicted $0.8$; the separation is small ($\hat\Delta=0.13$, as on Office-Home), and in three of the five seeds two groups are merged into one ($K=3$, ARI $0.62$--$0.70$), while the other two seeds recover the groups (ARI $0.93$ and $1.00$). DisCo-CFL is 2.2 points below the oracle in accuracy, and four baselines are more accurate: FedCCFA (90.6\% against 89.4\%, but with a 9-point lower concept accuracy), MTCFL (90.5\%), Local (89.6\%) and FedAvg-FT (89.5\%).

\paragraph{Summary.} Across the seven scenarios the hierarchical comparison gives DisCo-CFL a higher mean accuracy than every baseline, with intervals that exclude zero ($+1.2$ points against FL+HC, the closest, to $+10.7$ against FedAvg; Table~\ref{tab:hier}, part c), and a higher concept accuracy by $4.0$--$27.9$ points. Its failure rate is 23\%, against 54--100\% for the baselines (Table~\ref{tab:failure}). The outcomes follow the separation diagnostic of Section~\ref{sec:theory}: recovery succeeds where $\hat\Delta\ge0.24$ and the within-group linkage stays above $\tau$, and fails where $\hat\Delta$ is near or below $1-\tau$ or where noisy signatures with many classes push the within-group linkage below $\tau$.
